Words and History

Most of the vocabulary around gender and sexuality is built from a small kit of Greek and Latin parts. Learn the kit, and you can take apart words you've never met. Then: eight moments in the history of LGBTQIA+ rights — guess each year before you see it. Nothing you type leaves this device until you press Submit at the end.

1 · The morpheme kit

Read this once, properly — everything in section 2 is built from it.

homo-same (Greek) — homosexual: attracted to the same gender hetero-different, other (Greek) — heterosexual: attracted to a different gender bi-two (Latin) — bisexual: attracted to more than one gender pan-all (Greek) — pansexual: attraction regardless of gender a-without, not (Greek) — asexual: little or no sexual attraction trans-across, beyond (Latin) — transgender: gender is different from the sex presumed at birth. Same prefix as transport, translate — something crossing over. cis-on this side of (Latin) — cisgender: gender matches the sex presumed at birth. Chemists have used cis-/trans- as opposites for two centuries; the word isn't an insult, it's the other half of the pair. inter-between (Latin) — intersex: born with sex characteristics between typical definitions of male and female bodies -sexual / -romanticthe kind of attraction — who you want, versus who you fall for. They can differ: biromantic asexual is a meaningful, buildable phrase. -phobiafear or aversion (Greek) — homophobia, transphobia: hostility toward, not literal fear

The near-miss to avoid: these parts describe who someone is or who they're drawn to — identity and attraction are separate axes. Trans answers "what is your gender?"; homo/hetero/bi/pan/a answer "who are you attracted to?". A trans woman attracted to men is straight.

2 · Build the word

Type the word each definition describes. The kit above stays visible — using it is the point. Each word needs two correct answers to clear; a wrong answer sends it back into the queue. "Cleared" here means answered correctly twice this session, nothing grander.

3 · Take one apart

You've probably never met the word panromantic. Using only the kit, write what it must mean.

pan (all) + romantic (romantic attraction) → someone who can fall for people regardless of gender.

A strong answer names both parts and puts them together — not just "likes everyone", which misses that -romantic is specifically about falling for people, not sexual attraction and not liking. If your sentence did that, you can now decode aromantic, demisexual-style coinages, and anything else this kit produces.

4 · Eight moments — guess the year first

For each event, commit to a year before revealing. You won't be scored on closeness — the point is noticing whether these things happened longer ago, or more recently, than you assumed. Most people are wrong in an interesting direction.

5 · Two numbers from the research

About 4 in 10 said their school was supportive, and about 4 in 10 reported verbal harassment in the previous 12 months (Writing Themselves In 4, La Trobe University, 2021 — ~6,400 young Australians). Feeling supported at school was strongly linked with better mental health.

Whatever you guessed, notice the gap between your number and the real one — that gap is what days like Wear it Purple exist to close.
